Reply: 
 We thank the reviewer for these detailed and constructive observations. We address each point in turn.
 **(1) On "removing" surface modification.** Perhaps the term "removed" might not be accurate, but we also did not find within the manusript that we used of such phrase as "remove surface modification". Surface modification effects are not corrected for in constructing the CPF; rather, they are **avoided** through two complementary strategies. 
First, representative units were selected to be as geologically homogeneous as possible, with units exhibiting obvious partial resurfacing — such as transection by tectonic-like features or gradual crater density gradients (discussed in original Sections~5.1--5.3, revised Section 5.1.1--5.1.3) — excluded from CPF construction. 
Second, for each retained unit, only the diameter ranges judged reliably preserved are used: for sparsely cratered smooth plains, the small-crater range is retained as it is least affected by saturation or large-impact contamination; for heavily cratered units, the large-crater range is retained as small craters in these regions are more susceptible to preferential removal by saturation and tectonic overprinting. A formal procedure to identify the cut-off diameter $D_*$ and correct crater counts for partial resurfacing is described in a subsequent companion paper, where it is applied to derive first solidification and resurfacing ages for individual units. For the CPF construction presented here, such correction is deliberately avoided in favour of selecting units and diameter ranges where modification effects are minimal.

 **(2) On binning choice and polynomial degree.** The 0.05 in $\log D$ bin width is exactly one-third of the conventional $\sqrt{2}D$ binning ($\approx$0.15 in $\log D$), providing three times the diameter resolution and 34 bins over our diameter range compared to 12. This finer resolution is necessary given that some units contain as few as 34 craters, where coarser binning would produce highly uncertain or empty bins. We verified that the CPF shape is consistent when $\sqrt{2}D$ binning is applied, with the polynomial fit remaining within uncertainties; the finer binning does not introduce spurious modulations but rather better resolves genuine features. Regarding the 10th-degree polynomial: lower-degree polynomials were tested but produced unphysical behaviour — upward bending at the large-diameter end or downward bending at the small-diameter end — outside the well-sampled range, whereas the 10th-degree fit maintains a continuous downward slope across the full 800~m--35~km range. These behaviours matter because the CPF is used to fit isochrones across the full diameter range, and unphysical end behaviour would bias age estimates at the extremes.

 **(3) On Figure~5B.** Figure~5B is not intended to represent the CPF or its construction. It presents raw, unbinned crater size-frequency measurements for six terrain-type representatives to illustrate the global heterogeneity in crater density across Enceladus. The modulations visible in Fig.~5B reflect a mix of local resurfacing signatures and the unbinned Poisson noise of raw counts, and are not representative of the normalised composite envelope used to construct the CPF.

 **(4) On slope modulations.** Slope modulations in the crater SFD may arise from two sources: local effects due to partial resurfacing within individual units, and potential global effects reflecting the intrinsic impactor SFD or a globally acting crater removal mechanism. By combining measurements from multiple carefully selected units through the envelope-building approach, local modulations are smoothed out in the composite. Whether any residual modulation in the CPF reflects a global signal — such as variations in the impactor population or viscous relaxation — requires dedicated analysis including crater counts on other Saturnian moons and geophysical modelling of crater removal rates; this is beyond the scope of the current study and is left for future work.

 **(5) On the misleading sentence.** The sentence mentoined (orginal ln 626-627) has been revised to accurately state that the envelope-building approach minimises — rather than isolates — the influence of observational conditions and surface modification by retaining only reliably preserved diameter ranges per unit.

 **(6) On binning vs.\ no binning.** Fitting the polynomial directly to unbinned, raw cumulative counts is not advisable: individual crater detections contribute step-function increments to the cumulative SFD, and for units with low counts, Poisson noise dominates at all diameters, making a polynomial fit highly unstable and sensitive to individual craters. Some level of binning is therefore necessary to produce a smooth, well-conditioned dataset for fitting. The 0.05 $\log D$ binning represents a deliberate compromise: fine enough to preserve meaningful diameter resolution, but sufficient to average over Poisson fluctuations within each bin. A formal goodness-of-fit comparison between binning choices is beyond the scope of this paper, but the consistency of the polynomial shape across binning strategies provides confidence in the robustness of the result.

Ln 430: Why add resolution and lighting to the distinction? Is it possible that some of smaller split units are really more a part of a larger single unit? How does that change the results?
Reply: We thank the reviewer for this important question. Imaging-driven subdivision is scientifically necessary because a geologically coherent region captured by 
Cassini at different resolutions exhibits an artificial SFD flattening at small diameters in its lower-resolution portion --- indistinguishable from geological 
resurfacing without careful accounting. For each unit, a reliable minimum diameter limit (the lower end of the thershold diamater) is identified from the image resolution (and also relative size to the observed tectonic features), and crater bins below this limit are excluded from the CPF fitting. Instrumentally split sub-units of the same geological history are expected to yield mutually consistent SFDs, but only well above this limit, which is set by the poorer-resolution constituent image. The CPF derivation is therefore not sensitive to whether such areas are treated as one or two units, provided the resolution-driven diameter cutoff is correctly applied.
